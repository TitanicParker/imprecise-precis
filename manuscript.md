# How a Clinical Record Creates a Future
## A plain-language forensic handbook on referral scope, clinical investigation, explanatory reconstruction, and documentary inheritance

> **Central proposition**
>
> A broad GP referral entered hospital through an acute-medical pathway and was reduced to a narrower neurological précis. Neurology then performed substantial and sophisticated medicine inside that narrower frame. The final discharge package, however, had to account for a broader body of information than the narrowed investigation had directly resolved. The distance between the problem originally referred and the problem actually investigated is therefore the space in which the later explanatory work appears.
>
> In plain language:
>
> **the investigation answered the précis; the discharge synthesis had to answer the referral.**
>
> The central forensic question is whether the final document answered that larger problem by renewed medical discrimination, or by explanation that carried more weight than the underlying investigation had earned.

---

# 1. Why this case is best understood as an information-flow problem

This case can be described medically, legally, ethically, or administratively. But the most illuminating starting point is simpler:

**What happened to the problem as it moved through the system?**

The sequence matters because the same patient can be described differently at different stages without anyone necessarily lying, fabricating, or even noticing that the clinical mandate has changed.

The relevant stages are:

```text
GP TELEPHONE CONTACT
(contents unknown)
        ↓
WRITTEN GP REFERRAL THROUGH MAU/AMU
broad integrated lower-limb and movement problem
        ↓
HOSPITAL REFERRAL PRÉCIS
narrower neurological problem
        ↓
ADMISSION / INITIAL NEUROLOGY ASSESSMENT
strong Parkinsonian findings
        ↓
08:00 COMPLEX CASE MEETING THE NEXT MORNING
rapid multidisciplinary commitment around the problem then in view
        ↓
REMAINDER OF ADMISSION
clinical work continues within the narrowed neurological frame;
the original feet–gait–clawing mandate is not visibly restored
        ↓
10 JULY MORNING PROTEST
patient identifies that the original foot problem has not been dealt with
and asks for senior neurological review
        ↓
LESS THAN 24 HOURS TO PHYSICAL DISCHARGE
the broader GP mandate is now back in view,
but the preceding admission has not contained equivalent relationship-specific investigation
        ↓
DISCHARGE SYNTHESIS
the broader problem is made coherent through explanatory partition and disposition
        ↓
PLAN
"As per above"
        ↓
FUTURE RECORD
later clinicians inherit not only facts, but a ready-made structure for thinking
```

The case therefore turns on a distinction that is easy to miss:

> **The information set can become broad again without the problem set becoming broad again.**

A hospital may eventually possess many facts about the feet, gait, tone, pain, dystonic posture, alcohol history, and structural abnormalities. But possession of those facts is not the same thing as having made the relationship among them the object of fresh clinical investigation.

That is the organising idea of this handbook.

# 2. The five categories every reader must keep separate

Before examining the record, it is useful to distinguish five things that clinical documents often mix together.

## Observation

What somebody actually saw, measured, or demonstrated.

Examples in this case include severe rigidity, bradykinesia, an abnormal gait, a footdrop-like appearance judged suggestive of dystonia rather than true weakness, pes planus, and hammertoes.

## Patient history

What the patient reported about symptoms, timing, work, pain, alcohol, tension, and change over time.

History can be highly informative, but it is not the same thing as a clinician demonstrating biological independence between two symptom groups.

## Interpretation

What the clinician thought the observations and history meant.

For example, deciding that a lower-limb appearance is dystonic rather than a true footdrop is interpretation grounded in examination.

## Hypothesis

A proposed explanation that remains possible but unproved.

“Perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy” is the clearest example.

## Disposition

What happens next: treatment, referral, follow-up, allocation to a specialty, or incorporation into a plan.

A crucial lesson in this case is:

> **A hypothesis can remain weak as a diagnosis while becoming strong as a management tool.**

That is why “perhaps” does not mean “irrelevant.”

# 3. What the GP actually referred

The original GP referral is broader than a simple query of Parkinsonism.

The GP records an unusual festinant and stiff gait in a patient attending with bilateral foot problems. The feet are described as not being in a good state. The referral goes on to include difficulty initiating movement, increased tone, possible cogwheeling, inability to relax, toe clawing, worsening dexterity, and functional decline.

The GP’s actual question is open:

> `?parkinsonian or other underlying diagnosis`

The important point is not that the GP had already solved the case. The opposite is true.

The referral presents an **integrated problem**:

```text
painful / abnormal feet
        +
abnormal gait
        +
toe clawing
        +
increased tone
        +
difficulty initiating movement
        +
declining dexterity and function
        ↓
What underlying process explains this?
```

At this stage, the relationships are part of the clinical problem.

The GP has not already decided:

```text
Parkinsonism over here
+
unrelated structural/peripheral feet over there
```

Nor does the referral present peripheral neuropathy as an established second diagnosis.

That matters because a referral is not merely a list of symptoms. It is a **mandate for inquiry**.

# 4. The first compression: the hospital referral précis

The referral entered through the Medical Assessment Unit / Acute Medical Unit rather than directly through a Neurology outpatient pathway.

Before the written referral, the GP had also telephoned ahead. The record establishes the contact but not the content of the call. Nothing should be inferred about what was said.

The hospital’s formal referral précis is markedly narrower than the written GP referral.

It carries forward the neurological motor features:

- difficulty initiating movement;
- increased tone and stiffness;
- cogwheeling.

But the formal précis does not carry forward with equal visibility:

- bilateral foot problems;
- poor foot condition;
- corns/calluses;
- the patient’s gait-foot attribution;
- toe clawing;
- the original integrated feet–gait relationship.

This may have an ordinary explanation. Acute-medical triage often compresses long referrals into the features most obviously relevant to immediate specialty assessment.

That possible innocence is important.

But it only explains **how narrowing may have happened**.

It does not answer the later question:

> **What happened once the fuller information became available?**

That distinction protects the analysis from becoming simplistic.

The initial problem may not be wrongdoing at all.

It may be compression.

The forensic significance lies in what that compression did next.

# 5. The 08:00 problem: early multidisciplinary commitment

The patient was admitted on Monday 3 July 2017.

The Neurology Complex Case Meeting began at 08:00 the next morning, Tuesday 4 July.

That chronology matters because it means the meeting occurred very early in the admission, after the first day/night rather than after a mature reconstruction of the whole case.

We do not have native minutes of that meeting.

We do not know:

- which documents were physically reviewed;
- who presented the case;
- which findings had already been elicited;
- whether the full GP letter was read;
- whether toe clawing was discussed;
- whether the painful feet were discussed;
- whether the feet–dystonia relationship was raised.

But timing and surviving output permit a narrower inference.

The meeting appears to have developed a sophisticated neurological architecture:

- likely Parkinson’s disease with atypical features;
- recessive/genetic consideration;
- imaging;
- treatment strategy.

What is not visible in the surviving formulation is a reconstruction of:

```text
toe clawing
↔ hammertoes
↔ dystonic posture
↔ painful feet
↔ abnormal gait
```

This yields one of the most important teaching propositions in the case:

> **A team can become very sophisticated very quickly about the wrong-sized question.**

That is not a criticism of expertise.

It is a warning about **problem definition**.

Expert reasoning only becomes as broad as the question placed before it.

# 6. What Neurology investigated well

It is essential not to caricature this admission.

Neurology did substantial work.

The record documents:

- markedly increased tone;
- rigidity;
- bradykinesia;
- reduced arm swing;
- a striking abnormal gait;
- a footdrop-like appearance judged suggestive of dystonic posture rather than true footdrop;
- pes planus;
- hammertoes.

The neurological syndrome then receives extensive development.

Young-onset Parkinsonism becomes the principal conclusion.

DaT imaging is pursued.

A recessive/genetic cause is considered.

The dystonic-looking lower-limb sign is not ignored. It becomes useful neurological evidence. It helps support the atypical/young-onset Parkinsonian inquiry and appears in the DaT-related reasoning.

In other words, the Parkinsonian question undergoes a visible chain:

```text
question
→ examination
→ discrimination
→ impression
→ imaging/genetics
→ management
```

That chain is strong.

This case is therefore not about the hospital failing to recognise Parkinsonism.

It is about the **relationship between what was recognised neurologically and what happened to the painful feet**.

# 7. The missing relationship

The admission establishes coexistence.

By the time the principal neurological work is developed, the record contains:

- a major Parkinsonian syndrome;
- a dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon;
- painful feet;
- toe-clawing history;
- structural foot abnormalities;
- historical differences between pain and leg tension.

What the surviving record does not show with comparable depth is an examination directed at questions such as:

- Did the dystonic posture reproduce the foot pain?
- Was toe clawing dynamic or fixed?
- Were hammertoes structurally separate from the earlier toe-clawing phenomenon?
- Did neurological posture change loading through the feet?
- Did structural disease explain the abnormal gait, contribute to it, or coexist with it?
- Did the lower-limb dystonia contribute to pain without being the sole cause?
- Was there objective evidence of a peripheral sensory disorder?
- Could several mechanisms coexist?

The critical distinction is:

> **Coexistence was observed; independence was not equivalently demonstrated.**

That does not prove the two territories belonged to one cause.

It means the record does not display the same evidential depth in separating them as it displays in establishing Parkinsonism.

---

# 8. Information set versus problem set

This distinction is central.

By later in the admission, the record contains a rich set of facts about the feet.

The history restores:

- corns;
- calluses;
- fallen arches;
- pain;
- symptom course;
- occupational effects;
- alcohol history.

The examination adds:

- pes planus;
- hammertoes.

So it would be wrong to say that the feet simply disappeared.

They did not.

What appears to disappear is the **integrated question**.

This can be expressed simply:

```text
INFORMATION SET
What facts exist somewhere in the chart?

versus

PROBLEM SET
What relationship is the team actively trying to solve?
```

The information set becomes broad.

The active problem set appears to remain narrower.

That is why the final document can be factually rich while still being investigation-poor in one specific relational dimension.

---

# 9. The investigation–explanation gap

This is the heart of the case.

An investigation asks questions by gathering and discriminating evidence.

An explanation organises existing evidence into a coherent account.

Medicine requires both.

The forensic problem appears when the explanatory reach exceeds the visible discriminating work beneath it.

The crucial transition in Document 04 can be represented as:

```text
pain / burning
        ↓
possibly completely separate
        ↓
perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy
        ↓
possible alcohol contribution
        ↓
Not clear
        ↓
patient has "markedly divorced" the phenomena
        ↓
Podiatry
        ↓
As per above
```

The question is not whether every phrase is unreasonable.

Many are explicitly cautious.

The question is:

> **What work does the sequence perform that the examination itself had not performed?**

The answer is considerable.

It creates:

- a boundary;
- a mechanism;
- a possible cause;
- uncertainty that does not destroy the mechanism;
- patient-attributed support for separation;
- a service destination;
- a portable forward plan.

The key transformation is:

> **unresolved relationship → organised uncertainty**

---

# 10. The distance between referral and précis

The most important new way of understanding the case is to treat the difference between the GP referral and the hospital précis as an **explanatory deficit**.

The GP referral asks a broad question.

The hospital précis asks a narrower one.

Neurology investigates the narrower problem strongly.

The final discharge synthesis must nevertheless account for the broader record.

That creates a structural burden.

The greater the difference between:

```text
what the GP referred
```

and

```text
what the hospital actually investigated as its working problem
```

the more explanatory work is later required to make the finished discharge account look complete.

This does not mean every later sentence is defensive.

It means the final document faces a real structural problem:

> **How do you write a complete account of a broader case when the clinical investigation was concentrated on a narrower mandate?**

There are two broad ways to solve that problem.

## Route A — reopen the medicine

The service could explicitly restore the broader relationship question:

```text
painful feet
↔ toe clawing
↔ dystonic lower limb
↔ gait
↔ structure
```

and investigate it further.

## Route B — widen the explanation

The service can leave the prior examination intact and build a narrative capable of assigning the residual territory:

```text
separate
→ peripheral
→ perhaps neuropathic
→ perhaps alcohol-related
→ uncertain
→ patient also distinguishes it
→ Podiatry
```

The documentary record is substantially more consistent with **Route B** than with a visibly documented reopening of the relationship-specific examination.

That is the central forensic asymmetry.

# 12. The full loop: from scope error to manageable partition

The mechanism is now clearer if the admission is read as one continuous loop.

The broad GP referral is narrowed at entry. The neurological investigation then proceeds under that narrower mandate. The Complex Case Meeting gives the resulting neurological formulation rapid multidisciplinary authority. The admission continues inside that frame.

The important point is that the broader referral does **not** appear to be progressively restored during the admission.

The original integrated question remains outside the active clinical mandate until the patient’s **10 July morning Protest**, less than 24 hours before physical discharge.

The Protest therefore does more than reintroduce “the feet.”

It reintroduces the **scope of the original referral**.

It effectively says:

> **The problem that has been solved is not the whole problem that was referred.**

At that point, the hospital faces a serious reconciliation problem.

The preceding week has already produced a sophisticated neurological diagnosis and management architecture. But the patient has now exposed that the investigation did not exhaust the original presenting problem.

The system then has two broad options.

## Option 1 — preserve the discrepancy as an unresolved clinical problem

The record could say, in substance:

> Young-Onset Parkinsonism has been established. A dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon has been observed. The patient also has painful structural foot disease and earlier toe-clawing. The relationship among these findings remains unresolved and requires further neurological assessment.

That would preserve the fact that the original referral had not been fully answered.

## Option 2 — reorganise the residual territory into a separate, manageable pathway

The final synthesis instead develops a structure in which:

```text
DYSTONIC LOWER LIMB
        ↓
neurological sign
        ↓
Parkinsonism
        ↓
DaT / genetics / Neurology
```

while:

```text
PAINFUL FEET
        ↓
possibly completely separate
        ↓
possible peripheral neuropathy
        ↓
possible alcohol contribution
        ↓
Not clear
        ↓
patient represented as strongly distinguishing the phenomena
        ↓
Podiatry
```

This is the crucial move.

The record does **not** make the dystonic lower limb itself into neuropathy.

It does something more consequential:

> **it preserves the dystonic lower limb inside the Parkinsonian model while giving the painful-foot territory a different explanatory future.**

Once that split exists, the earlier narrowing no longer has to remain visible as a failed or incomplete investigation.

The completed account can instead read as though the admission discovered several coexisting components, considered plausible explanations for each, and produced an organised forward plan.

That is the full loop:

```text
broad referral
→ narrowed précis
→ sophisticated investigation of the narrowed problem
→ rapid multidisciplinary commitment
→ patient exposes the omitted scope
→ no equivalent reopening of relationship-specific investigation
→ residual territory is retrospectively partitioned
→ each partition receives a mechanism and destination
→ the final record becomes administratively and clinically manageable
```

The documentary effect is therefore stronger than simply saying:

> explanation replaced investigation.

A more complete formulation is:

> **the consequence of the narrowing is transformed from a possible scope error into a differential-diagnosis and service-allocation problem.**

That distinction matters.

The completed document does not preserve:

> “we followed too narrow a clinical mandate and therefore left part of the original problem under-investigated.”

Instead, it preserves:

> “there were different symptom territories; one belonged to the Parkinsonian problem, another may have been separate/peripheral, uncertainty was acknowledged, and the residual feet had an appropriate destination.”

That is what makes the final state neurologically, administratively, and from a governance perspective **manageable**.

It also explains why the explanatory sequence has such high functional yield.

The peripheral construction gives the residual territory:

- a symptom quality;
- a boundary;
- a biomedical mechanism;
- a possible cause;
- explicit uncertainty;
- apparent support from the patient’s own distinction;
- a service destination;
- and incorporation into the formal plan.

Nothing in that requires neuropathy to be proved.

The construction only has to be plausible enough to make the unresolved territory portable.

## The key proviso: effect is visible; motive remains a separate question

The finished document has the effect of preventing the narrowing from appearing as an explicit unresolved error.

That effect can be described from the record.

A stronger proposition — that someone consciously decided that the admission “must not be allowed to look like a mistake” — is a question of intent and provenance.

The disciplined formulation is therefore:

> **The final synthesis converts the consequence of an apparent scope error into a clinically and administratively manageable partition, without preserving the scope error itself as an active unresolved problem.**

If later production evidence shows that this transformation was consciously undertaken in response to the 10 July Protest, that would materially strengthen the inference about purpose.

But the documentary mechanism does not depend on proving that motive.

# 12. The neuropathy construction: why it matters without being proved

Peripheral neuropathy is important in this case not because the document definitively diagnoses it.

It does not.

Its importance is functional.

Once available, it solves several documentary and management problems at once.

## It gives the residual pain a mechanism

If painful feet are to be treated as separate from the main neurological syndrome, something still has to explain them.

“Perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy” supplies a medically recognisable category.

## It gives the mechanism a cause

Alcohol history then provides a possible aetiological route.

A mechanism with a possible cause is more coherent than an unexplained residual symptom.

## It allows uncertainty to remain

The phrases `perhaps` and `Not clear` preserve provisional status.

This is a crucial lesson:

> **Uncertain does not mean inactive.**

A hypothesis can remain tentative while still shaping referral, expectation, management, and future interpretation.

## It gives the feet somewhere to go

Podiatry is independently appropriate for genuine structural foot disease.

But in the final architecture, Podiatry also supplies an operational destination for the residual foot territory.

That creates the appearance of management completeness.

## It reduces pressure to reopen the relationship

Once both sides have destinations:

```text
Parkinsonism / dystonic lower limb → Neurology

painful structural feet / possible neuropathy → Podiatry
```

the question connecting the two becomes less operationally urgent.

That is the functional power of the construction.

---

# 13. `Not clear`: uncertainty placed inside the alternative route

`Not clear` appears cautious.

And it is cautious.

But location matters.

The document does not prominently say:

> Not clear whether the painful feet relate to the dystonic lower-limb abnormality.

Instead, uncertainty appears after the separate/peripheral/alcohol route has already been introduced.

That means the uncertainty is largely positioned **inside the alternative model**.

The model becomes:

```text
perhaps separate
perhaps neuropathy
perhaps alcohol-related
not clear
```

The original relationship question is not restored with equal visibility.

This is why conditional language can be unusually durable.

The mechanism remains possible enough to organise the case, but not definite enough to be easily falsified.

---

# 14. The patient-attribution problem

Earlier history records that the patient came to regard foot pain and leg tension as “not the same problem.”

That is meaningful history.

But it is not the same proposition as biological independence.

The later synthesis states that the patient had, “in his own mind,” “markedly divorced” the two territories.

The shift is important:

```text
not the same problem
```

becomes

```text
markedly divorced
```

That stronger phrasing makes the partition look more congruent with the patient’s own conceptual model.

For future readers, this can matter greatly.

A clinician may be less inclined to reopen a relationship if the record suggests that both clinician and patient had already strongly separated the phenomena.

The forensic question is not whether the later wording is necessarily false.

It is:

> **How much stronger is the final attribution than the underlying patient report, and what work does that strengthening perform?**

---

# 15. Alcohol as a reusable explanatory fact

Alcohol performs several different functions across the record.

It appears as:

1. background history;
2. self-management or “de-tensioning” behaviour;
3. something the patient feared might have caused his condition;
4. something the neurologist did not want the patient to believe had caused the Parkinsonian disorder;
5. possible etiological support for peripheral neuropathy;
6. later, a future-facing variable in the continuing neuropathy hypothesis.

This does not automatically make the reasoning inconsistent.

Different diseases can have different causal relationships with alcohol.

But the forensic point is that one historical fact becomes an **explanatory resource** capable of doing different jobs in different compartments.

That means the record should be especially clear about:

- what is known;
- what is biologically plausible;
- what is speculative;
- what evidence would strengthen or weaken the causal inference.

Without that clarity, causal history can become sticky.

# 17. Why toe clawing matters so much

Toe clawing is one of the cleanest tracers of the information loss.

It appears in the GP referral alongside increased tone, abnormal gait, and difficulty relaxing.

It disappears from the hospital referral précis.

Later, the specialist record contains:

- hammertoes;
- a dystonic-looking lower limb;
- painful feet.

Yet the surviving reasoning does not visibly reconstruct:

```text
toe clawing
↔ hammertoes
↔ dystonic posture
↔ painful feet
```

That omission does not prove the patient was never examined in detail.

But it raises an exact forensic question:

> **Was the patient examined under the full mandate of the GP referral, or under the narrower mandate produced by the hospital précis?**

That is much more precise than saying “the feet were not examined.”

# 18. The role of the 10 July Protest

The core documentary mechanism can be identified from the records alone:

```text
broad referral
→ narrowed précis
→ strong investigation of the narrowed neurological problem
→ broader final synthesis
```

But the Protest is not peripheral to the actual chronology.

The patient’s account places the bedside teaching interaction on the **morning of 10 July 2017**, with four trainees present. During that interaction he raised the unresolved bilateral foot pain, asked for the supervising neurologist, and challenged the adequacy of proceeding without addressing the problem that had brought him into hospital. Physical discharge occurred on **11 July 2017**.

That timing matters.

The Protest therefore occurs:

```text
after triage and the narrowed referral précis
→ after the 4 July 08:00 Complex Case Meeting
→ after the main Parkinsonian investigation and management frame had developed
→ while the patient was still an inpatient
→ before physical discharge
→ before the final documentary package was fully settled
```

Its significance is not merely that the patient believed his feet had not been dealt with.

It is more exact:

> **the patient contemporaneously identified that the problem actually solved by the hospital was narrower than the problem with which he had arrived.**

That creates a live clinical fork on 10 July:

```text
recognise the scope mismatch
→ reopen the feet–gait–dystonia relationship clinically

or

recognise the scope mismatch
→ preserve the existing medical investigation
→ broaden the final explanation instead
```

The surviving record does not show a comparably broadened relationship-specific examination after the 10 July challenge.

What does broaden is the final synthesis.

That makes the Protest relevant to **notice**: by 10 July the unresolved breadth of the original problem had been directly put back before the treating neurologist while there was still time, in principle, for senior neurological review before physical discharge.

The remaining provenance question is not whether the Protest belongs in the chronology. It is:

> **which parts of the final explanatory architecture already existed before 10 July, and which were added, modified, or strengthened after the unresolved scope problem had been explicitly raised?**


# 19. What `As per above` does

Document 04 is unusually long and explicitly consolidative.

It contains:

- observations;
- history;
- diagnostic conclusions;
- hypotheses;
- uncertainty;
- causal possibilities;
- counselling;
- treatment;
- referrals;
- genetic discussion.

Then the formal plan says:

> `Plan/Recommendations: As per above.`

This is important because the narrative itself becomes plan-bearing.

The document does not finally sort its content into:

```text
Established
Possible
Unresolved
Management only
Needs reassessment
```

Instead, it incorporates the preceding architecture by reference.

This can be described as:

> **incorporation without epistemic reclassification**

In plain language:

**everything travels forward together without being freshly labelled according to how certain it is.**

That makes the explanatory architecture highly portable.

# 20. What Document 04 actually creates

Document 04 creates more than a record of an admission.

It creates a **future starting point**.

A later clinician can reasonably inherit the impression that:

- Parkinsonism was thoroughly investigated;
- the unusual lower-limb movement was recognised;
- the feet were also considered;
- neuropathy was considered;
- alcohol was considered;
- the patient himself strongly distinguished the symptoms;
- Podiatry was allocated the feet;
- genetics were considered;
- there was a clear plan.

Every one of those impressions has some textual basis.

That is precisely why the combined architecture is powerful.

The danger is not crude misinformation.

The danger is:

> **an appearance of completed relationship-analysis produced by multiple individually defensible components.**

The unresolved relationship can therefore disappear from future curiosity without ever being explicitly declared resolved.

# 21. 2025: the inherited architecture under pressure

By March 2025, the neuropathy hypothesis remains conditional.

The clinician still writes in terms equivalent to:

> if there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here

Alcohol abstinence is still allowed to organise the expected future of that possible neuropathy.

At the same time, the same 2025 record acknowledges:

- the feet are a major concern;
- Sinemet changes the feelings in the feet;
- dystonia can be painful;
- increasing Sinemet would probably provide further pain relief, albeit with dyskinesia risks;
- observable abnormal foot/walking movements occur.

The patient is no longer pharmacologically naïve.

His 2025 phenomenology is treatment-modified.

Therefore, later symptom description cannot automatically be treated as a pure retrospective test of the untreated 2017 state.

The 2025 complaint then explicitly challenges the old framing.

The clinician’s rebuttal defends the historical model partly by emphasising that neuropathy had always been conditional.

That creates a revealing tension:

> **The same conditional wording can keep a hypothesis available clinically and later demonstrate that it was never treated as certain.**

That is one reason uncertain hypotheses can survive for a very long time.

# 22. PRKN: what later genetics changes and what it does not

Later testing confirmed pathogenic PRKN/Parkin variants.

The eventual specialist interpretation placed Parkinsonism and dystonia within Parkin disease.

This does not prove:

- every painful foot symptom was dystonia;
- structural foot disease was irrelevant;
- neuropathy was impossible;
- alcohol could not have contributed to anything.

What it does is stress-test the original partition.

Neurology had already:

- observed a dystonic-looking lower limb;
- considered PRKN/PARK2.

Later genetics confirms that dystonia belonged centrally to the molecular disease.

That makes one old question more important:

> **How well was the painful-foot territory separated from that neurological sign before the two were given different futures?**

The later diagnosis should not be projected backwards as knowledge the team possessed.

But it can legitimately test whether an earlier unresolved relationship was insufficiently preserved.

# 23. The central medical issue

The medical issue is not whether provisional diagnoses are allowed.

They are essential to medicine.

The issue is whether a provisional hypothesis has a proper lifecycle.

A sound record should make clear:

1. why a hypothesis was raised;
2. what supports it;
3. what argues against it;
4. what evidence would strengthen or weaken it;
5. what management work it is being used to perform;
6. what should trigger reconsideration.

The key medical question here is:

> **Was the feet–dystonia relationship sufficiently investigated before the painful-foot territory was allowed to travel forward as a separate peripheral problem?**

That is the precise expert question.

---

# 24. The central ethical issue

The ethical issue is not merely diagnostic correctness.

It concerns transparency, revisability, and fidelity to the patient’s experience.

A patient can accurately describe two sensations as different without establishing that they arise from independent disease processes.

A clinician can appropriately use a tentative diagnosis without allowing it to become a durable substitute for an unresolved relationship.

Ethically, the record should distinguish:

- what the patient said;
- what the clinician inferred;
- what remained unknown;
- what was done for practical management.

The case therefore raises a simple ethical principle:

> **Do not let a workable explanation become more durable than the uncertainty that gave rise to it.**

---

# 25. The central governance issue

The governance problem begins before diagnosis.

It begins with **scope**.

A broad referral can be compressed.

Compression can be reasonable.

But the compression must remain reversible.

The key governance questions are:

- Was the original referral preserved and accessible?
- Who created the précis?
- Was the scope difference recognised?
- What question entered the Complex Case Meeting?
- Did the meeting review the original referral or only the active hospital problem?
- Were unresolved relationships preserved explicitly?
- Were provisional hypotheses given review triggers?
- Could future clinicians tell what remained open?
- Did service allocation substitute for integrated review?

The central governance lesson is:

> **Expert review cannot compensate for an unrecognised loss of problem scope. The scope itself has to be audited.**

Those require jurisdiction-specific law and appropriate expert evidence.

But the record now identifies the exact legal questions that matter.

## Duty / scope of clinical responsibility

What problem was actually presented to the hospital?

What did the receiving and specialist services know or have available?

Did responsibility include reconstructing the original referral once the broader facts were available?

## Breach / standard of care

Was it reasonable to investigate the neurological syndrome intensively while leaving the relationship between dystonic lower-limb findings and painful structural feet without equivalent discrimination?

Was the peripheral-neuropathy pathway sufficiently evidenced before it became part of forward management?

Those are expert medical questions.

## Documentation

If the historical letters are later relied upon as the audit trail of reasoning, their omissions and internal distinctions become legally relevant evidence.

A later explanation cannot automatically prove what was actually investigated earlier.

## Causation

Even if a documentary or clinical failure were established, legal causation still requires proof that a different approach would probably have altered outcome.

Later Sinemet response and PRKN confirmation may be relevant, but neither alone proves what would have happened in 2017.

## Notice

If the Protest is independently established, it may become relevant to when the scope problem was brought directly to the treating team’s attention.

But the underlying documentary case does not depend upon it.

# 31. Final synthesis

The case is now best understood as a problem of **scope, timing, and documentary substitution**.

The GP sent a broad lower-limb problem.

The acute-medical pathway compressed that problem into a narrower neurological mandate.

Neurology then did impressive work within that mandate. Parkinsonism was recognised, a dystonic-looking lower limb was identified, sophisticated imaging and genetic reasoning followed, and a Complex Case Meeting gave the neurological formulation multidisciplinary authority very early in the admission.

The broader foot facts do not appear to have been restored as an active clinical mandate during the admission itself. The decisive re-widening occurs with the 10 July morning Protest, less than 24 hours before physical discharge, when the patient directly identifies that the original foot problem has not been dealt with and asks for senior neurological review.

At that point, the hospital is no longer dealing merely with an unresolved symptom. It is confronted with the fact that the problem actually investigated was narrower than the problem originally referred.

The final synthesis then broadens back toward the full referral, but the medical investigation does not visibly broaden in parallel.

The surviving record does not show the relationship between painful structural feet, toe-clawing history, and the dystonic neurological lower limb being investigated with the same depth as the Parkinsonian syndrome itself.

Yet the final discharge document had to account for the whole case.

That is where the explanatory architecture becomes visible.

The residual territory is made manageable through separation, neuropathy, alcohol, uncertainty, patient attribution, Podiatry, and incorporation into the plan.

The strongest source-grounded formulation is therefore:

> **The distance between the original GP referral and the hospital referral précis became the explanatory burden of the final discharge synthesis. The admission investigated the narrowed problem strongly and remained inside that frame until the 10 July Protest exposed the missing scope. Less than 24 hours before physical discharge, the broader mandate was back in view, but the preceding week of relationship-specific investigation did not exist to support it. The final synthesis therefore made the residual territory manageable by partition: the dystonic lower limb remained neurological, while the painful feet were given a possible peripheral-neuropathy/alcohol/Podiatry future. In that way, the consequence of the narrowing ceased to appear as an explicit scope error and became a clinically organised differential-and-disposition problem.**

That does not prove intentional concealment.

It does not prove that neuropathy was false.

It does not prove that every foot symptom was dystonic.

It identifies something more fundamental:

> **a difference between what the medicine directly established and what the final record made sufficiently coherent to carry forward.**

That difference is where the medical, legal, ethical, and governance questions now reside.
